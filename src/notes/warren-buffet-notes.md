In investing, one of the tools we have for measuring this risk is called the debt-to-equity (D/E) ratio.

Warren Buffett likes a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.5 or lower.

When we talk about current asset, it is something that we expect will turn into cash within the next twelve months.

A current liability, on the other hand, is something that we need to pay within the next twelve months. When we compare our current assets and current liabilities, we are actually looking at the current ratio.

Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities

Warren Buffett generally likes a current ratio above 1.5. That means that he wants his companies to receive $1.5 every time a debt of $1 must be paid within the next twelve months. The idea is straightforward: if a company always receives more cash than it pays out, the company can meet its short-term debt obligations at any time.
