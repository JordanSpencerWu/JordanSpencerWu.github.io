Distinguish between ruminating and problem-solving. Occasionally you might have a useful insight while ruminating, but mostly it’s avoidance coping. To shift from rumination to improvement mode, ask yourself, “What’s the best choice right now, given the reality of the situation?”

Train your brain to become nonstick. As soon as you notice you’re ruminating, try to distract yourself for a few minutes. Engage in an activity that’s short and mentally absorbing but not extraordinarily difficult.

Physical activity, such as jogging or walking, can also calm a mind that’s prone to rumination. Meditation or yoga can be especially helpful for protecting yourself from sticky thoughts and learning not to over-engage with them.

Check your thinking for errors. Sometimes rumination is triggered by cognitive errors. The catch-22 is that you’re not likely to be very good at detecting distorted thinking when you’re ruminating, since it clouds thinking.
